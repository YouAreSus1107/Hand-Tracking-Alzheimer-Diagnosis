Finger Tapping Test

screening_tests/finger_tapping.py

Tap your index finger to your thumb rhythmically.

Big & Fast (20 s)Paced (30 s) 1 handCV% headline metric

How It Works

Two modes share the same detection engine. Both start with the same calibration. They differ in how you pace the taps.

Big & Fast

20 s · self-paced · primary
1

Calibrate

Pick your webcam or IP camera. MediaPipe tracks 21 points on your hand. A short open-and-close warm-up sets the starting tap threshold for your hand size, and from there it follows the excursion you are actually making — so taps still count as they get shallower through the run.

2

Tap big & fast

Tap your index finger to your thumb as big and as fast as you can for 20 seconds. The app measures tip-to-tip distance on every frame. Full-excursion tapping like this runs at 2 to 3 taps per second in healthy adults. Our runs land around 1 to 2, and that gap is still open.

3

Score

The headline score is CV% of your inter-tap intervals. You also get tap frequency, amplitude variability, and speed decrement.

Paced Rhythm

30 s · 1 Hz metronome
1

Calibrate

Same camera setup and the same open-and-close warm-up to set your tap threshold.

2

Warm up to the beat

You get 5 seconds of practice with the metronome. Beeps play 200 ms early to cancel out audio delay. They run on a fixed grid so they never drift.

3

Tap on each beep

Tap once per beep for 30 seconds, one beep per second. Staying consistent matters more than being perfectly on time.

4

Score

The headline score is CV%, your rhythm consistency. Beat-sync SD shows how close your taps land to each beep. The first 3 warm-up taps are dropped.

This is the MDS-UPDRS finger-tapping movement — thumb to index through the widest range you can manage, hand in the air. It is not the desk-tapping test, where one finger taps a key with the wrist resting and the amplitude does not matter. That distinction sets the whole scale: desk tapping runs at 5–7 taps per second, full-excursion tapping at 2.4–3.6, falling with age. A rate that looks alarmingly slow against the wrong test is unremarkable against the right one.

Metrics Measured

Rhythm variability (CV%)

The test captures the Standard Deviation of inter-tap intervals. The scale-invariant prime biomarker.

<15% typical · 15–25% monitor · >25% follow-up*
IIV elevated across neurodegenerative groups — Roalf 2018, TapTalk 2024

Tap frequency (Hz)

Taps per second = 1000 ÷ mean inter-tap interval.

Big & Fast runs ~1–2 Hz · norm 2.4–2.7 Hz at 50+
MediaPipe frequency 90.3% within ±1 Hz of a Polhemus sensor — TapTalk 2024, which measures 2.6 Hz on this instruction; Kwon 2022 2.69 Hz at 50–69. We see 1–2; gap still open.

Speed decrement (%/s)

Slope of the instantaneous tap rate over the trial. This captures fatigue slowdown.

negative = slowing
“Big & Fast” decrement measure — TapTalk 2024

Amplitude CV (%)

Variability of the valley-to-peak opening excursion from tap to tap.

span-normalized per tap
Magnetic-sensor tap amplitude/regularity — Suzumura 2022

Beat-sync SD (ms) · paced only

Standard Deviation of each tap’s latency to its nearest beep (matched within a ±500 ms window).

lower = tighter to the beat
Project-specific rhythm metric — no cited normative source.

Minimum 6 taps; every result carries a confidence level. *The 15% and 25% cut-offs are ours, not the literature’s — no normative cohort yet. Context, not diagnosis.

Research Papers

Quantitative Finger Tapping in MCI, Alzheimer's & Parkinson's

Roalf et al., 2018 · J Neurol 265:1365–1375 · PMC5992087

They tested 302 people (131 AD, 63 PD, 46 MCI, 62 healthy) with a light-diode finger tapper. Variability in the gaps between taps was higher in every neurodegenerative group, and it separated the groups better than tap count or average speed. We cite it because our headline CV% / IIV metric comes from it.

TapTalk: Smartphone Motor + Speech Testing Across 20 Devices

Li et al., 2024 · Alzheimer's & Dementia: DADM · PMC11496774

They checked MediaPipe hand tracking against Polhemus electromagnetic sensors across 31 people and 20 phone models. 90.3% of tap-frequency readings landed within ±1 Hz of the sensor. We cite it because it shows a plain camera is accurate enough for this test.

Directly validates our approachRead paper

TAS Test: Webcam Hand Movements Predict Cognition

Li et al., 2024 · Alzheimer's & Dementia: DADM · PMC10809289

404 adults with no cognitive symptoms did a 10-second webcam tapping test at home. Adding their hand-motor features improved predictions of memory, executive function, and working memory. We cite it because it shows the test works unsupervised at home.

At-home webcam validationRead paper

Age-Related Differences in Finger Tapping

Kwon et al., 2022 · Technology and Health Care 30(S1) · PMC9028619

83 healthy adults aged 20 to 89 tapped index finger to thumb for 15 seconds “as rapidly as possible and with as large amplitude as possible” — our instruction, word for word — with a sensor on the finger. Rates fell from 3.53 Hz in the twenties to 2.69 at 50–69 and 2.36 at 70–89. We cite it because it is the closest thing to a normative rate for this test, and it is nowhere near the desk-tapping figure.

Spiral Tracing Test

Trace a spiral in the air with your fingertip.

40 s test1 hand On-screen guideAir tracing

How It Works

1

Guide Display

A spiral guide appears on screen. Hold your hand in front of the camera with your index finger pointed out.

2

Trace the Spiral

Trace the spiral from the center outwards with your fingertip, at whatever pace feels comfortable. There is no dot to chase. The app records your raw fingertip position on every frame.

3

Movement Analysis

The app looks at how your speed changed during the trace. It scores how smooth and steady the movement was, not how closely you matched a target.

4

Smoothness Scoring

The headline score is a smoothness index called SPARC. You also get velocity CV%, normalized jerk, a tremor readout, and how much of the spiral you finished.

Metrics Measured

Smoothness Index (SPARC)

Spectral Arc Length of the speed profile (Balasubramanian et al. 2015), mapped 0–100. The headline: higher means smoother, more automatic movement.

Velocity CV%

Coefficient of variation of movement velocity. Irregular speed profiles are a hallmark of neurodegeneration.

Normalized Jerk

Smoothness of movement trajectory. Jerkier movements suggest reduced motor automaticity.

Completion

Percentage of the spiral successfully traced. Lower completion may indicate motor fatigue or difficulty.

Active Ratio

Proportion of time the finger was actively moving vs. paused. Hesitations can indicate planning difficulty.

Research Papers

Digitized Archimedes Spiral Drawing Test — Scoping Review

Wang et al., 2025 · Movement Disorders Clinical Practice

A review of how the digitized spiral test is used across MS, ataxia, dystonia, cerebellar disease, MCI, and Alzheimer's. We cite it because it confirms spiral tracing is an established, measurable test.

Validates the test direction

Spiral + Wave Drawing with Explainable CNN for PD

2024/2025 · Springer

A deep-learning model classifies Parkinson's from spiral and wave drawings. Its saliency maps show which parts of the drawing drove the call. We cite it as an example of keeping automated scoring explainable.

Explainable AI approach

Tapping + Spiral for Tremor Differential Diagnosis

NCT06378619 · ClinicalTrials.gov

An active trial that combines tapping and spiral drawing with machine learning to tell tremor types apart. We cite it as precedent for combining our own tests into one classifier.

Eye Movement Test

screening_tests/oculomotor_test.py

A webcam-only pro/anti-saccade task. A dot flashes to the left or right. In Part 1 you look toward it. In Part 2 you look away from it. The headline metric is the anti-saccade error rate: how often your eyes get pulled to the dot anyway. It measures inhibitory control, and it is one of the earliest signs of Alzheimer's that can be measured.

Pro baseline (16 trials)Anti-saccade (24 trials) 3-point gaze calibrationError-rate headline

How It Works

Both blocks share one gaze engine. MediaPipe tracks your iris and measures its position against your eye corners, so small head movements do not throw it off. A short calibration maps that signal to the screen.

1

Calibrate

Look at three dots: center, then left, then right. This maps your iris signal to screen positions and sets a center deadband from your own tracking noise. Keep your head still and move only your eyes.

2

Part 1 — Look toward

Keep your eyes on the center cross. When a dot appears, look at it, then look back. 16 scored trials set your normal saccade speed as a baseline.

3

Part 2 — Look away

When the dot appears, look to the opposite side. This is the main block. 24 trials measure how often inhibition fails and how fast the correct looks are.

4

Score

The headline score is your anti-saccade error rate. Anti − Pro latency cancels out the fixed camera and display delay. You also get the corrected-error rate and data-quality checks. Each block uses a gap paradigm and throws out guesses faster than 90 ms.

Metrics Measured

Anti-saccade error rate (%)

Share of anti-saccade trials whose first eye movement went toward the dot — a failure of inhibition.

<20% typical · 20–40% monitor · >40% follow-up*
Best AD discriminator (SMD 1.59) — Opwonya 2022

Anti − Pro latency (ms)

Anti-saccade latency minus the prosaccade baseline. The subtraction cancels the fixed, unknown camera-exposure + display-render offset.

the robust latency readout
Standardised protocol — Antoniades 2013

Corrected-error rate (%)

Anti-saccade errors that the eyes then self-corrected toward the right side within the hold window — inhibition failure vs error-monitoring.

counted separately
Corrected vs uncorrected errors — Crawford 2005

Latency CV (%) · validity gates

Variability of correct-trial latency, plus valid-trial counts and face-visible ratio that decide whether a run is scoreable.

honest data-quality reporting
Excludes express/anticipatory (<90 ms) saccades.

A run needs at least 12 valid anti-saccade trials to be scored. This is a short screening form (40 trials vs the clinical 40–60 per block). A webcam at 30–60 fps resolves saccade direction robustly but not velocity or trajectory. *Bands are anchored to published healthy (~6%) and AD (~25%) error rates (core/gaze/metrics.py) — not a clinical cut-off. Performance depends on lighting, camera quality, glasses, and attention.

Research Papers

Saccadic Eye Movement in MCI and Alzheimer's — Meta-Analysis

Opwonya et al., 2022 · Neuropsychology Review 32(2):193–227 · PMC9090874

A meta-analysis pooling 27 task-condition datasets. The anti-saccade error rate showed a large effect separating AD from controls (SMD 1.59) and a moderate one for MCI (0.55), and it beat prosaccade measures at telling patients from controls. We cite it because our headline metric comes from it.

Source of our headline metricRead paper

Inhibitory Control of Saccades and Cognitive Impairment in AD

Crawford et al., 2005 · Biological Psychiatry 57(9):1052–1060 · PMID 15860346

AD patients left about 10 times more anti-saccade errors uncorrected than controls (25.4% mean), and the error rate tracked dementia severity. We cite it because it anchors the high end of our scoring bands.

Anchors the AD error bandRead paper

An Internationally Standardised Antisaccade Protocol

Antoniades et al., 2013 · Vision Research 84:1–5 · PMID 23474300

The reference protocol for clinical saccade testing. We follow its rules: prosaccade block first, a gap between the cross disappearing and the dot appearing, express saccades excluded, and corrected errors counted apart from uncorrected ones.

Hand Tracking / UDP Broadcast

core/hand_tracking.py

Streams all 21 hand landmarks (x, y, z) in real time over UDP to 127.0.0.1:5052. It was built for a Unity hand-mirroring receiver. It now serves as the raw landmark backbone for the screening tests.

Live stream2 hands UDP :505221 landmarks

How It Works

1

Camera Selection

A console window asks for your camera source. Choose a local webcam or paste an IP camera stream URL.

2

Landmark Detection

MediaPipe detects up to 2 hands at once. It returns 21 3D landmarks per hand on every frame.

3

UDP Broadcast

Every frame, the pixel-scaled coordinates are packed as L:[x1,y1,z1,...] and sent over UDP. One packet per detected hand.

4

Live Visualization

An OpenCV window shows the camera feed with the hand skeleton drawn on top. Press q to quit.

Data Output

21 Landmarks

Wrist, thumb (4), index (4), middle (4), ring (4), pinky (4) — full hand skeleton per the MediaPipe topology.

3D Coordinates

Each landmark has x, y (pixel-scaled) and z (relative depth). One-Euro filtering smooths x/y; z is left raw.

UDP Format

Newline-delimited packets: L:[x1,y1,z1,x2,y2,z2,...] per hand, broadcast to localhost:5052.

Preprocessing

CLAHE + sharpening via preprocess_for_mediapipe improves detection in challenging lighting.
