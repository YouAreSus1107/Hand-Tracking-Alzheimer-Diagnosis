Tested Against Real Data

1 · Does it measure tapping correctly?

We ran our finger tapping test on EHWGesture, a public dataset from Politecnico di Torino presented at ICCV 2025. Every video was filmed next to a 120 fps motion capture system tracking the thumb and index finger. That gives the true time of every tap, so we can check ours against it.

99.7%of taps found in held-out volunteers
0.7points median CV% error against motion capture
45 / 47recordings placed in the same result band
598 / 600irregular runs flagged at the 15% cut-off

264 recordings · 22 healthy volunteers · 3 tapping speeds · tap rate r = 0.98

  1. Keep a test set aside

    We followed the dataset's own split and held 5 volunteers back. No setting was changed to fit them.

  2. Match every tap

    Each camera tap was paired with its motion capture tap. Ours land about 18 ms after finger contact, with 16 ms of spread.

  3. Stress test irregular tapping

    We stretched cycles in 150 real recordings to set CV% anywhere from 6% to 48%. The median read back stayed within 3 points.

2 · Does it tell patients apart?

We ran the same test, unchanged, on HUBU-FIS, 234 phone videos from 75 people with Parkinson's and 43 controls. A neurologist rated every hand on the UPDRS finger tapping item, from 0 (normal) to 3 (clearly impaired).

0.86AUC separating UPDRS 2 to 3 from UPDRS 0
93%of unimpaired hands graded Typical
73%of impaired hands flagged Monitor or Follow-up
0.47rank correlation between our CV% and the neurologist's grade

234 videos · 118 people · 95% CI for AUC 0.77 to 0.94 · 229 of 234 scored

How This Test Changed

Every change to how we score tapping, and the reason for it. Newest at the bottom.

  1. Jul 2026Start

    Start from Roalf 2018

    Roalf and colleagues found that uneven gaps between taps set MCI, Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s apart from healthy people better than speed did. That made CV% of tap intervals our headline number. The first version tapped to a 1 Hz metronome.

  2. Jul 2026Fixed

    Lighter smoothing

    The landmark smoothing rounded off the moment the fingers meet, which hid real taps when tapping fast. We lightened it.

  3. Aug 2026Changed

    Big & Fast becomes the main mode

    An audit found the papers we cite used tapping as big and fast as possible, not a metronome. We added that mode, following TapTalk and Suzumura, and kept the metronome as a second mode.

  4. Aug 2026Fixed

    Thresholds fit each hand

    One fixed touch threshold failed for large or distant hands, and a 300 ms lockout blocked fast taps. A short warm-up now learns each person’s open and closed hand.

  5. Aug 2026Added

    Confidence on every score

    A CV% from 5 intervals is not as trustworthy as one from 60. Every result now carries a confidence level and a 95% interval (Miller 1991), and the test runs 20 s to collect enough intervals.

  6. Aug 2026Fixed

    Thresholds follow the tapping

    Real taps are smaller than the warm-up and shrink over the run, so fixed thresholds missed the shallow ones. One missed tap doubles an interval and made healthy runs read Follow-up. Thresholds now follow the last 3 seconds, with a floor so a still hand never counts.

  7. Sep 2026Fixed

    Honest speed expectations

    The 5 to 7 taps per second we assumed belongs to desk key tapping. For this thumb and index movement people of 50 and over manage about 2.4 to 2.7 (Kwon 2022). A speed floor built on the wrong figure rejected about 70% of real runs, so it became a fixed 0.5 taps per second.

  8. Sep 2026Added

    Pause when the hand leaves

    If the hand drops out of frame the test pauses, and the interval across the gap is left out. It measures the pause, not the rhythm.

  9. Sep 2026Tested

    Checked against motion capture

    On EHWGesture, with 120 fps markers on 22 healthy volunteers, we found 99.7% of taps in held-out volunteers. It also showed 15 of 217 healthy recordings graded Follow-up, mostly where the camera lost the fingers.

  10. Sep 2026Rejected

    Four detector ideas dropped

    Re-timing taps, a lower re-open threshold, re-arming after each dip and filling long gaps each helped one check and broke another, for example by counting a still hand as tapping.

  11. Sep 2026Added

    A recording quality gate

    To stop those false alarms, runs with many half closures or wildly uneven openings were asked to redo. False Follow-ups fell from 15 to 5, and none of our own 85 sessions were affected.

  12. Sep 2026Removed

    The gate hid patients

    On HUBU-FIS, 234 videos of Parkinson’s patients and controls, the gate held back 10 of 26 severe patients, because uneven openings are a sign of Parkinson’s. We removed it and accepted the extra false alarms.

  13. Sep 2026Tested

    Tested on patients

    With the gate gone, CV% separates impaired from unimpaired hands with an AUC of 0.86. It grades 91% of unimpaired hands Typical and flags 73% of impaired ones.

  14. Sep 2026Fixed

    Forgive one missed tap

    Fast tappers were marked down for one slip, often our own missed tap. If the only long gap in a steady run holds a full closure we failed to count, it is now forgiven, and it may lift the result by one band at most. Pauses and half closures still count. No patient lost their flag, and 93% of unimpaired hands now read Typical.

  15. Sep 2026Added

    Compared with your own usual

    Some patients tap slowly but evenly, which rhythm alone cannot see. A fixed speed limit would flag healthy people who simply tap at a relaxed pace at home. So each run is now compared with the same person's earlier runs. If your last five runs are under 55% of your usual speed, a Typical result becomes Monitor. Opening size and shrinking openings are shown but do not change the result.

  16. Sep 2026Rejected

    More measures tested, none added yet

    We tested extra rules for the patients rhythm alone misses. A speed limit caught 85% of impaired hands instead of 73%, but at home it would flag a quarter of healthy runs, so it waits for a supervised mode. Uneven opening size added two patients at no cost, a gain too small to act on yet. Shrinking and small openings flagged more healthy hands than patients.

This is the MDS-UPDRS finger-tapping movement — thumb to index through the widest range you can manage, hand in the air. It is not the desk-tapping test, where one finger taps a key with the wrist resting and the amplitude does not matter. That distinction sets the whole scale: desk tapping runs at 5–7 taps per second, full-excursion tapping at 2.4–3.6, falling with age. A rate that looks alarmingly slow against the wrong test is unremarkable against the right one.

Metrics Measured

Rhythm variability (CV%)

The test captures the Standard Deviation of inter-tap intervals. The scale-invariant prime biomarker.

<15% typical · 15–25% monitor · >25% follow-up*
IIV elevated across neurodegenerative groups — Roalf 2018, TapTalk 2024

Tap frequency (Hz)

Taps per second = 1000 ÷ mean inter-tap interval.

Big & Fast runs ~1–2 Hz · norm 2.4–2.7 Hz at 50+
MediaPipe frequency 90.3% within ±1 Hz of a Polhemus sensor — TapTalk 2024, which measures 2.6 Hz on this instruction; Kwon 2022 2.69 Hz at 50–69. We see 1–2; gap still open.

Speed decrement (%/s)

Slope of the instantaneous tap rate over the trial. This captures fatigue slowdown.

negative = slowing
“Big & Fast” decrement measure — TapTalk 2024

Amplitude CV (%)

Variability of the valley-to-peak opening excursion from tap to tap.

span-normalized per tap
Magnetic-sensor tap amplitude/regularity — Suzumura 2022

Beat-sync SD (ms) · paced only

Standard Deviation of each tap’s latency to its nearest beep (matched within a ±500 ms window).

lower = tighter to the beat
Project-specific rhythm metric — no cited normative source.

Minimum 6 taps; every result carries a confidence level. *The 15% and 25% cut-offs are ours, not the literature’s — no normative cohort yet. Context, not diagnosis.

Metrics Measured

Smoothness Index (SPARC)

Spectral Arc Length of the speed profile (Balasubramanian et al. 2015), mapped 0–100. The headline: higher means smoother, more automatic movement.

Velocity CV%

Coefficient of variation of movement velocity. Irregular speed profiles are a hallmark of neurodegeneration.

Normalized Jerk

Smoothness of movement trajectory. Jerkier movements suggest reduced motor automaticity.

Completion

Percentage of the spiral successfully traced. Lower completion may indicate motor fatigue or difficulty.

Active Ratio

Proportion of time the finger was actively moving vs. paused. Hesitations can indicate planning difficulty.

What We Measure

A real recording

Why latency reads slow. It is timed from the frame that draws the dot to the frame that sees your eyes move. The webcam hands each frame over late, the screen shows the dot late, and frames arrive only every ~35 ms. Together that adds roughly 150–200 ms to every trial, however fast you react, so a prosaccade that takes 150–200 ms in a lab reads about 350 ms here. The delay is the same on every trial, so Anti − Pro cancels it: read that one (roughly 50–100 ms in healthy adults). Compare absolute latencies only between runs on the same camera and screen.

screening_tests/oculomotor_test.py

A webcam-only pro/anti-saccade task. A dot flashes to the left or right. In Part 1 you look toward it. In Part 2 you look away from it. The headline metric is the anti-saccade error rate: how often your eyes get pulled to the dot anyway. It measures inhibitory control, and it is one of the earliest signs of Alzheimer's that can be measured.

Both blocks share one gaze engine. MediaPipe tracks your iris and measures its position against your eye corners, so small head movements do not throw it off. A short calibration maps that signal to the screen.

Metrics Measured

Anti-saccade error rate (%)

Share of anti-saccade trials whose first eye movement went toward the dot — a failure of inhibition.

<20% typical · 20–40% monitor · >40% follow-up*
Best AD discriminator (SMD 1.59) — Opwonya 2022

Anti − Pro latency (ms)

Anti-saccade latency minus the prosaccade baseline. The subtraction cancels the fixed, unknown camera-exposure + display-render offset.

the robust latency readout
Standardised protocol — Antoniades 2013

Corrected-error rate (%)

Anti-saccade errors that the eyes then self-corrected toward the right side within the hold window — inhibition failure vs error-monitoring.

counted separately
Corrected vs uncorrected errors — Crawford 2005

Latency CV (%) · validity gates

Variability of correct-trial latency, plus valid-trial counts and face-visible ratio that decide whether a run is scoreable.

honest data-quality reporting
Excludes express/anticipatory (<90 ms) saccades.

A run needs at least 12 valid anti-saccade trials to be scored. This is a short screening form (40 trials vs the clinical 40–60 per block). A webcam at 30–60 fps resolves saccade direction robustly but not velocity or trajectory. *Bands are anchored to published healthy (~6%) and AD (~25%) error rates (core/gaze/metrics.py) — not a clinical cut-off. Performance depends on lighting, camera quality, glasses, and attention.

screening_tests/speech_test.py

A microphone-only diadochokinesis task: repeat pa-ta-ka as fast and as evenly as you can for 8 seconds. It is the speech counterpart of finger tapping and is scored the same way. The headline metric is rhythm variability: how much the gaps between syllables vary.

Each syllable starts with a closure (near silence) and ends in a loud vowel, so the loudness of the recording rises and falls once per syllable. The test finds those peaks and times the gaps between them.

The second part is a sustained vowel: hold "ahhh" at a comfortable pitch for 6 seconds. Praat measures how much the voice wobbles from one vocal-fold cycle to the next, on the steady middle of the vowel only. The headline there is jitter.

Metrics Measured

Rhythm variability (CV%)

Standard deviation of the gaps between syllables divided by their mean. Scale-free, so a fast and a slow speaker can be compared.

<15% typical · 15–25% monitor · >25% follow-up*
Same shape as the tapping headline

Syllable rate (/s)

Syllables per second. Speech rate separates Alzheimer's groups from controls with a moderate effect in pooled studies.

SMD ≈ 0.66 for speech rate
Cohen et al. 2025, meta-analysis

nPVI

Normalised pairwise variability index: how different each gap is from the next one. Unlike CV%, it ignores a slow drift in speed.

the standard DDK rhythm index
JSLHR 2022

Order errors (%) · experimental

With the phoneme model installed: the share of syllable-to-syllable steps that broke the pa → ta → ka order. Recorded but not shown for now: on test speech the model reported 15–57% errors on clean pa-ta-ka, so it needs checking against real voices first.

Jitter (%)

Cycle-to-cycle variation in the length of each vocal-fold vibration, measured by Praat on a held vowel. Reported higher in mild Alzheimer's than in controls.

<1.04% typical · 1.04–2.08% monitor · >2.08% follow-up*
1.04% = MDVP threshold (Praat manual)

Shimmer · HNR · vocal tremor

Shimmer is the same idea for loudness; HNR is how much of the voice is tone rather than breath noise; vocal tremor is a slow 2–10 Hz wave in the pitch, the voice's version of a hand tremor.

supporting readouts, no bands
Measured by Praat through praat-parselmouth

A run needs at least 10 syllables and a voice at least 12 dB above the room noise to be scored. *The bands are provisional: they match the tapping test's bands because no DDK-specific cut-off has been fitted yet (core/speech/tasks.py). Results depend on the microphone, the room and effort. Audio is analysed and then discarded: only the loudness envelope and the syllable times are saved.

The vowel is scored only if at least 2.5 s of it is steadily voiced and the voice is at least 15 dB above the room. *The jitter bands are provisional: the lower edge is the MDVP pathology threshold, the upper edge is not from a source, and the evidence linking voice acoustics to Alzheimer's is thinner than for speech rate. Only the pitch contour is saved.

screening_tests/tremor_test.py

Three 20-second holds with both hands in view: resting in the lap palms up, then palms down, then with the arms held out. The test looks for a rhythmic shake between 3.5 and 12 Hz in each hand. The headline is the tremor's frequency, which a webcam measures reliably, with its size given as an estimate.

Rest tremor is checked with the hands in the lap, fully supported, the way a neurologist examines it, and both sides of the hand are seen. A laptop's own camera on the table cannot see your lap, so aim a webcam down at it before you start.

Metrics Measured

Tremor frequency (Hz)

The strongest rhythm in the 3.5–12 Hz band. Rest tremor in Parkinson's runs at about 4–6 Hz; essential and physiological tremor run faster.

within ~0.2 Hz of an accelerometer
Sci Rep 2025 · Williams 2021

Tremor-band movement (%)

RMS movement at the tremor frequency, as a share of your own hand length, so hand size and distance from the camera cancel out. Also converted to an approximate centimetre figure.

an estimate — not clinical-grade on video
npj Digit Med 2024

Left / right ratio

How much larger the rest movement is in one hand than the other. Parkinsonian tremor usually starts on one side.

both hands recorded together
MDS-UPDRS 3.17

Camera vs glove (Hz)

With the sensor glove plugged in, its motion sensor records every hold too. The gap between the glove's peak and the camera's peak is how this test is being validated.

glove gyro, 100 Hz
docs/tests/TREMOR_TEST_PLAN.md

A hold is scored when at least 8 seconds of it had the hand fully in view and still. The band the test can see depends on the frame rate: at the 60 frames a second of an external webcam it covers the full 3.5-12 Hz, while at the ~16 fps of a busy laptop camera it stops near 7.6 Hz, which covers rest tremor but not the faster kinds. The detection thresholds are provisional (core/tremor/metrics.py). A tremor is not a sign of Alzheimer's disease; the test is here to catch a tremor that would distort the other motor tests, and to flag one that is worth a doctor's look.

The seated part of the walking test, for Parkinson's signs in the legs. It needs about a metre of floor and carries no fall risk: leg stamps (MDS-UPDRS 3.8), then five sit-to-stands (3.9), filmed side-on. The walking part is still to come.

The camera watches from the side, 2–3 m away, so it can see the knee lift and the hips rise. A phone streaming over Wi-Fi works through the camera chip's URL option. Before the first block you raise your right arm: that tells the test which leg is which on this camera. Only body landmarks are saved, never video.

Metrics Measured

Five sit-to-stands (s)

Time from the beep to the fifth full stand. Rising from a chair takes leg strength, balance and the movement speed Parkinson's slows. Failed attempts and pushing off with the hands are counted too, as the MDS-UPDRS scores them.

provisional bands: 13 s / 16 s
Bohannon 2006 · Duncan 2011

Leg stamps

Stamps per second and how high the knee lifts, as a share of thigh length, for each leg. Measured with the same detector as finger tapping, and so is the fade in lift over the ten seconds.

reported, not banded
MDS-UPDRS 3.8

Left vs right

Which leg is weaker, when the two differ by more than a fifth. Parkinson's usually starts on one side.

named only on a clear difference

Hand Tracking / UDP Broadcast

core/hand_tracking.py

A live landmark inspector. It streams all 21 hand landmarks (x, y, z) in real time over UDP to 127.0.0.1:5052 for any program listening there, and overlays the tap signal and raw coordinates so you can check what the tracker sees before a test.

Live stream2 hands UDP :505221 landmarks

How It Works

1

Camera Selection

Pick the camera with the camera chip beside the Launch button. It applies the next time the tool starts.

2

Landmark Detection

MediaPipe detects up to 2 hands at once. It returns 21 3D landmarks per hand on every frame.

3

UDP Broadcast

Every frame, each detected hand is sent as one UDP datagram, L:[x1,y1,z1,...] or R:[...].

4

Live Inspector

The window shows the skeleton, a panel per hand with open/closed state, taps and a distance trace, and a table of key landmark coordinates. Keys: r hides the table, c resets the tap counters, q or the window's close button quits.

Data Output

21 Landmarks

Wrist, thumb (4), index (4), middle (4), ring (4), pinky (4) — full hand skeleton per the MediaPipe topology.

3D Coordinates

x and y are pixels in the unmirrored camera frame; z is relative depth, scaled by the frame width. All three are sent as integers. One-Euro filtering smooths x/y; z is left raw.

UDP Format

One datagram per hand, no newline: L:[x1,y1,z1,x2,y2,z2,...] (63 values). The L/R prefix is MediaPipe's own label, which on this unmirrored frame names the user's real hand.

Preprocessing

CLAHE + sharpening via preprocess_for_mediapipe improves detection in challenging lighting.

Every channel drawn where it sits on the hand. The layout is the decided eleven-channel set — flex on the backs of four fingers, FSR402 on four fingertips, FlexiForce A401 on three palm pads — and only the channels the board’s banner declares light up. Motion — Onboard IMU

accelerometer + gyroscope, 100 Hz

The camera says where the hand is, at ~30 fps and only while it stays in frame. The IMU sits on the hand and says how it is moving, at 100 Hz, through occlusion and out of frame — which is what a 4–12 Hz tremor band needs. Spec: docs/glove/GLOVE_FIRMWARE_PLAN.md §5d.
